3. Data Preparation & Modeling — Python

The supplied Jupyter notebook uses Pandas and supporting libraries to load and inspect the dataset. The workflow includes:

  • Installing/importing required packages, including Pandas, gdown, PyMySQL and SQLAlchemy.
  • Loading the CSV and inspecting sample rows and descriptive statistics.
  • Checking missing values and filling missing review ratings with the median rating within each product category.
  • Standardizing column names to lowercase with underscores and renaming the purchase amount field to purchase_amount.
  • Creating age groups: Young Adult (up to 30), Adult (31–50), Middle aged (51–60), and Senior (61–100), based on the notebook's bin boundaries.
  • Mapping purchase-frequency labels to approximate day intervals: weekly 7, fortnightly/bi-weekly 14, monthly 30, quarterly/every 3 months 90, annually 365.
  • Checking the relationship between discount and promo-code fields, then dropping promo_code_used.
  • Exporting the prepared data to CSV.
The CSV supplied with this project already contains the engineered age_group and purchase_frequency_days fields and has no missing values.

4. Business Analysis — MySQL

The SQL script contains ten business questions. These queries aggregate and compare customer purchase records to support business interpretation.

Q1–Q5: Revenue, discounts, ratings and shipping
  1. Total revenue by gender.
  2. Customers who used a discount but spent at least the overall average purchase amount.
  3. Five products with the highest average review rating.
  4. Average purchase amount for Standard versus Express shipping.
  5. Subscriber and non-subscriber counts, average spend and total revenue.
Q6–Q10: Promotions, segments and product preferences
  1. Five products with the highest percentage of discounted purchases.
  2. Customer segmentation based on previous purchases: New (1), Returning (2–10), Loyal (more than 10).
  3. Top three purchased products within each category.
  4. Subscription status among repeat buyers with more than five previous purchases.
  5. Revenue contribution by age group.
SQL techniques demonstrated: GROUP BY, aggregate functions, subqueries, CASE expressions, common table expressions (CTEs), window functions (ROW_NUMBER), sorting and LIMIT.

7. Limitations & Interpretation Notes

  • The dataset contains one row per customer ID (3,900 unique IDs). The SQL script groups these records and uses previous-purchase counts; it does not contain a dated, multi-row transaction history.
  • Therefore, “revenue” in this report means the sum of the recorded purchase_amount field across customer records, not verified lifetime revenue.
  • The SQL file includes analysis queries but does not show a separate transaction-generation script. Treat transaction simulation as a stated project objective unless implemented elsewhere.
  • The supplied SQL defines “New” as exactly one previous purchase. A stricter first-time-customer definition (zero previous purchases) is not used by this query.
  • These are descriptive results. They show associations and differences, not causal effects.
  • The provided files do not specify a business currency conversion or a time period for the records; purchase amounts are presented as USD as indicated by the notebook's source field name.

8. Conclusion & Recommendations

The project demonstrates a practical analytics workflow across Python, MySQL and Power BI. It organizes customer data, answers defined business questions with SQL and summarizes customer and product patterns for business users.

  • Review category-level performance, particularly Clothing, which contributes the largest recorded revenue.
  • Evaluate discount effectiveness using margin and conversion data before expanding promotions.
  • Investigate the small average-spend difference between subscriber and non-subscriber groups with additional customer-level and time-based data.
  • Use repeat-purchase and loyalty segments to design retention campaigns, while validating the segment definitions with the business.
  • Extend the dataset with order IDs, transaction dates, quantities, costs and customer history to enable true cohort, retention and profitability analysis.
